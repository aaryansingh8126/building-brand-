§2 · BUSINESS DIAGNOSIS

Why a “good ROAS” can still leave the founder unhappy

Revenue ≠ profit

At ₹2,400 AOV with 25–63% discount badges, the list price the customer anchors on is higher than what gets banked. Every rupee of discount comes straight out of margin.

ROAS ≠ contribution margin

6× ROAS in a low-margin case (26.5% contribution before ads) leaves ₹59K a month after ads, before salaries and rent. In a high-margin case it leaves ₹2.18L. Same ROAS, a 3.7× difference in profit.

Orders ≠ quality customers

Sale-led acquisition attracts buyers who wait for the next sale. Unless second-order rate and time to second order are tracked by acquisition source, a cheap buyer can't be told apart from a good one.

New customers ≠ LTV

The 20% returning rate is the lever with the least wasted spend: those orders cost almost nothing in ads. Holding new orders at 200, each extra point of returning rate adds about 3 orders, or ₹7–8K a month. Moving from 20% to 28% adds about ₹67K.

Cheap CAC ≠ sustainable

₹400–500 CAC at ₹1L spend won't hold at ₹1.5L. Marginal CAC rises as Meta moves past the cheapest buyers. The question is the marginal ROAS on the extra ₹50K, not the average.

Scale ≠ more of the same

Frequency of 1.11 means the audience isn't saturated. The ceiling is creative volume and site conversion, not reach.

Rule: the garment supplies the saturated colour. Set, background and type stay in 1–2 neutrals from this palette. There's never more than one accent in a frame.

Typography for ads

  • Support: a clean sans (e.g. Manrope, Instrument Sans) for price, fabric, CTA
  • Max 2 text blocks per static, under 20% of the frame
  • Price set small and calm, never inside a starburst

Photography rules

  • Real Indian women, varied skin tones and body sizes, natural skin texture
  • Directional window light or soft studio key; avoid flat ring light
  • Architecture: jaali, sandstone, lime-plaster walls, arches, terrazzo, modern interiors
  • Movement in every set: dupatta lift, walk, turn, sleeve fall
  • One macro frame per shoot: thread, mirror, gota, neckline
  • Jewellery: one piece, max two

Layout grammar

  • Product and model take 60–70% of the frame
  • Negative space kept on one side (top or left) for type
  • Hierarchy: Hook → Product name → one benefit → CTA
  • Logo small, bottom corner, same place every time

Never

  • Stock photos, Canva templates, generic "Shop Now" banners
  • Over-retouched plastic skin, AI faces

  • More than ~12 words on a static

    GUARDRAILS · CRITICAL THINKING RULES

    Rules the team works under

    Never fabricate

    Competitor data, Ad Library data, margins, conversion rates, demographics or benchmarks. If a number is unknown, write "unknown" and model scenarios.

    Never over-build

    No 20-campaign accounts. No over-segmented small budgets. No blind Advantage+ or blind interests. Each has a stated reason.

    Never optimise for one number

    Not CTR alone, not ROAS alone. Reach isn't conversion and clicks aren't quality traffic.

    Never call a winner early

    Winners need spend floors, purchase evidence and replication (§25).

    Never fake urgency

    Only real stock, real drop dates and real dispatch deadlines.

    Never discount by default

    Every offer is judged on CM3 per order and its effect on repeat buying.
